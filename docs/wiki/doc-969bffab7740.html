The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum, 1900-04
Part of Works around L. Frank Baum

This document is a modernized fairy tale introduced by L. Frank Baum as a story meant to preserve wonder and joy without the heartaches and nightmares of older tales. It follows Dorothy, a Kansas girl swept away with her dog Toto when a cyclone carries her house to the land of the Munchkins. Her house kills the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing the Munchkins, and the Witch of the North gives Dorothy enchanted Silver Shoes and sends her along the yellow brick road to ask the Great Oz to help her return home. On the road Dorothy gains three companions: the Scarecrow, who wants brains; the Tin Woodman, who wants a heart after the Wicked Witch of the East turned him to tin; and the Cowardly Lion, who wants courage. Together they survive forests, gulfs, a river, deadly poppies, and other dangers, aided at times by field mice. At the Emerald City, Oz appears differently to each traveler and demands that they kill the Wicked Witch of the West before he will grant their wishes. In the Winkies’ country, the Witch attacks the group with wolves, crows, bees, and Winged Monkeys. Dorothy is captured, but she melts the Witch by throwing water on her, frees the Winkies, and reunites her companions. Back in the Emerald City, the travelers discover Oz is an ordinary man and a humbug who created frightening appearances with tricks. Still, he gives the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion objects that persuade them they have received brains, a heart, and courage. His attempt to take Dorothy home by hot-air balloon fails when Toto delays her and Oz floats away alone. Dorothy and her friends then travel south to Glinda, passing the Dainty China Country, helping the Lion become King of the Forest, and crossing the Hammer-Heads’ hill with the Winged Monkeys. Glinda reveals that Dorothy’s Silver Shoes could have carried her home all along. After arranging homes for her companions, Glinda tells Dorothy to use the shoes. Dorothy returns to Kansas with Toto, loses the shoes in the desert, and is joyfully welcomed by Aunt Em.

Introduction

The introduction discusses how folklore, legends, myths, and fairy tales have accompanied childhood and delighted children with fantastic stories. It argues that older fairy tales can now be considered historical because modern children prefer entertainment without frightening or unpleasant incidents. L. Frank Baum says that The Wonderful Wizard of Oz was written to be a modernized fairy tale retaining wonder and joy while leaving out heartaches and nightmares.

Chapter I

Dorothy lives with Uncle Henry and Aunt Em in a small, gray house on the Kansas prairies, where her black dog Toto keeps her cheerful. When a cyclone approaches, Uncle Henry goes to the livestock sheds, while Aunt Em tells Dorothy to run to the cyclone cellar. Dorothy is caught inside the house with Toto as the cyclone lifts it into the air and carries it away. After becoming less frightened, Dorothy and Toto fall asleep while the house sways in the wind.

Chapter II

Dorothy awakens after the cyclone sets her house down in the land of the Munchkins, where the Witch of the North and three Munchkins greet her. They explain that the house has killed the Wicked Witch of the East and freed the Munchkins from bondage. Unable to return home across the deserts, Dorothy is directed to travel along the yellow brick road to the City of Emeralds and ask Oz for help. The Witch of the North gives Dorothy enchanted silver shoes and protects her with a kiss before disappearing.

Chapter III

Dorothy prepares for and begins her journey from the Munchkins' country to the Emerald City, hoping the Great Oz will help her return to Kansas. She stays with Boq and learns that the journey will take many days and pass through dangerous places. Along the yellow brick road, Dorothy frees the Scarecrow from his pole and invites him to travel with her so Oz might give him brains.

Chapter IV

Dorothy and the Scarecrow travel along an increasingly rough yellow-brick road toward the Emerald City. The Scarecrow tells Dorothy how he was made, why he wants brains, and how crows discovered that he was stuffed with straw. They enter a dark forest and find a log-and-branch cottage where Dorothy and Toto sleep while the Scarecrow keeps watch.

Chapter V

Dorothy and the Scarecrow find the Tin Woodman rusted in the forest and rescue him by oiling his joints. The Tin Woodman joins them on their journey to the Emerald City, where he hopes the Great Oz will give him a heart, while the Scarecrow seeks brains and Dorothy seeks a way back to Kansas. The Tin Woodman tells how the Wicked Witch of the East caused his body to be replaced piece by piece with tin, leaving him without a heart. The travelers continue along the yellow brick road, but Dorothy worries that their bread is nearly gone.

Chapter VI

Dorothy, Toto, the Tin Woodman, and the Scarecrow travel through a dangerous forest toward the Emerald City. A Lion attacks them but admits that he is a coward and joins the group to ask Oz for courage. The Tin Woodman accidentally kills a beetle, cries until his jaws rust, and learns to walk more carefully.

Chapter VII

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion travel through the forest toward the Emerald City along the road of yellow brick. The Lion carries them across a first ditch, and the group crosses a second gulf on a tree bridge before escaping an attack by two Kalidahs. They reach a swiftly flowing river and begin building a raft, but it is unfinished when night falls.

Chapter VIII

Dorothy and her companions cross the river on a raft, but the current carries them away from the road of yellow brick and leaves the Scarecrow stranded on his pole. The Cowardly Lion and Tin Woodman help reach the shore, and a Stork rescues the Scarecrow. The travelers find themselves in a deadly field of poppies, whose scent puts Dorothy, Toto, and the Lion to sleep. The Scarecrow and Tin Woodman carry Dorothy and Toto to safety, but the Lion remains asleep among the flowers.

Chapter IX

The Tin Woodman kills a Wildcat that is chasing the Queen of all the Field Mice, and she promises that her people will serve him. At the Scarecrow’s suggestion, the Queen sends thousands of field mice with pieces of string to help rescue the Cowardly Lion from the poppy bed. The Tin Woodman builds a truck, and the mice harness themselves to it and pull the sleeping Lion into the fresh air. Dorothy wakes, thanks the mice, and waits with the Scarecrow and Tin Woodman for the Lion to awaken.

Chapter X

The Cowardly Lion recovers from the deadly poppies, and the travelers resume their journey along the road of yellow brick toward the Emerald City. They spend a night with a farming family, where they learn that the Great Oz is difficult to see and may appear in many forms. At the Emerald City entrance gate, the Guardian of the Gates admits them after fitting each traveler with locked green spectacles. He then leads them through the city toward the Palace.

Chapter XI

Dorothy and her companions arrive in the green City of Oz and are lodged in the Palace of Oz while waiting to see the Great Wizard. Oz appears to Dorothy as an enormous Head, to the Scarecrow as a lovely Lady, to the Tin Woodman as a terrible Beast, and to the Cowardly Lion as a Ball of Fire. Oz promises to grant each companion's wish and send Dorothy back to Kansas only after they bring proof that the Wicked Witch of the West is dead. The friends agree to journey to the land of the Winkies to seek and destroy the Witch.

Chapter XII

Dorothy and her companions travel west into the country of the Winkies, where the Wicked Witch of the West repeatedly sends creatures and slaves to destroy them. The Tin Woodman defeats forty wolves, the Scarecrow defeats forty crows, and the bees cannot harm the Tin Woodman, while the Winkies flee from the Cowardly Lion. Using the Golden Cap for the third and final time, the Wicked Witch summons the Winged Monkeys, who scatter the Scarecrow, batter the Tin Woodman, imprison the Lion, and carry Dorothy to the Witch’s castle. The Witch enslaves Dorothy and tries to steal both Silver Shoes, but Dorothy throws water on her, causing her to melt away.

Chapter XIII

Dorothy frees the Winkies after the Wicked Witch is melted and leads them in rescuing the Tin Woodman and the Scarecrow. The Winkies repair the Tin Woodman and restore the Scarecrow with clean straw. After reuniting, Dorothy and her friends decide to return to Oz to claim his promises. The travelers receive gifts from the Winkies and begin their journey to the Emerald City.

Chapter XIV

Dorothy and her companions become lost while walking east through the great fields toward the Emerald City. Dorothy uses the Golden Cap's charm to summon the Winged Monkeys, who carry the four travelers and Toto to the city. The King of the Winged Monkeys explains that his people must obey the Golden Cap because Gayelette cursed them to fulfill three commands for each owner. The Winged Monkeys set the travelers down safely at the Emerald City gate.

Chapter XV

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion return to the Emerald City after Dorothy melts the Wicked Witch of the West. In the Throne Room, they discover that Oz is an ordinary man and a humbug who used disguises, machinery, fire, and ventriloquism to appear powerful. Oz explains his history and admits that he cannot truly grant their requests, but he agrees to provide the Scarecrow with brains, the Tin Woodman with a heart, and the Cowardly Lion with courage. He promises to consider how Dorothy can return to Kansas if the travelers keep his secret.

Chapter XVI

The Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion each visit Oz to receive the brains, heart, and courage they believe they need. Oz gives the Scarecrow a mixture of bran, pins, needles, and straw, places a silk-and-sawdust heart in the Tin Woodman, and gives the Lion a drink from a green bottle. Each returns to the others pleased and convinced that the desired quality has been given to him. Oz privately admits that he is a humbug and worries that he cannot get Dorothy back to Kansas.

Chapter XVII

Dorothy helps Oz make a hot-air balloon so they can cross the desert and return toward Kansas. Oz plans to leave the Emerald City and tells its people that the Scarecrow will rule in his absence. When the balloon is launched, Dorothy is delayed while searching for Toto, and the ropes break before she can board. Oz rises into the sky alone, leaving Dorothy behind while the citizens remember him fondly.

Chapter XVIII

Dorothy grieves that Oz is gone and that she still cannot return to Kansas. The Scarecrow becomes ruler of the Emerald City, while the Tin Woodman and Lion are satisfied with what they received from Oz. The Winged Monkeys refuse to carry Dorothy across the desert, so the travelers learn that Glinda, the Witch of the South, may be able to help. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion decide to begin a journey to the Land of the South the next morning.

Chapter XIX

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, the Lion, and Toto leave the Emerald City and continue their journey to the Land of the South so Dorothy can return to Kansas. They travel through fields and a thick forest, where the first row of trees attacks them by throwing them back. The Tin Woodman chops through the trees so the travelers can pass, but they soon reach a high wall made of smooth white china.

Chapter XX

Dorothy, the Tin Woodman, the Scarecrow, the Lion, and Toto climb over a high wall and enter the Dainty China Country. The country contains tiny china houses, animals, and people who fear being broken. Dorothy meets a china princess, who explains that china people become stiff when taken away from their country. The travelers cross the country carefully and leave over another wall after the Lion accidentally smashes a china church.

Chapter XXI

Dorothy, the Lion, the Scarecrow, the Woodman, and Toto travel through a wild forest and find hundreds of animals holding a meeting. The forest animals tell the Lion that a huge spider-like monster has been eating them and ask him to become their king if he defeats it. The Lion attacks the sleeping great spider and kills it by knocking off its head. The animals make the Lion their King of the Forest, and he promises to return after Dorothy is safely on her way to Kansas.

Chapter XXII

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Lion, the Tin Woodman, and Toto reach the Hammer-Heads’ hill while traveling toward the Country of the Quadlings. The Hammer-Heads repel the Scarecrow and the Lion with their shooting heads, so Dorothy uses the Golden Cap to summon the Winged Monkeys and have them carry the travelers over the hill. In the Country of the Quadlings, the travelers receive food from a farmer’s wife and learn that Castle of Glinda is nearby. They reach the castle and are admitted to see Glinda.

Chapter XXIII

Dorothy tells Glinda the Good Witch about her arrival in Oz and asks to return to Kansas. Glinda accepts the Golden Cap and arranges for the Winged Monkeys to take the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion to their desired homes. She explains that Dorothy’s Silver Shoes can carry her anywhere in three steps. Dorothy uses them to return to Kansas, but the shoes fall off and are lost in the desert.

Chapter XXIV

Aunt Em is watering the cabbages when she sees Dorothy running toward her. Dorothy tells Aunt Em that she has come from the Land of Oz and that Toto is with her. Dorothy is glad to be home again, and Aunt Em welcomes her with kisses.
