In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Tin Woodman is a tin man and woodman who seeks a heart from the Great Oz, helps his companions through dangers, receives a silk, sawdust-stuffed heart, and wishes to rule the Winkies. In The Marvelous Land of Oz, Tin Woodman is Nick Chopper, Emperor of the Winkies, who welcomes the Scarecrow’s party, helps recapture the Emerald City, uses his mechanical skills and axe to aid his friends, and returns to his kingdom. In Ozma of Oz, Tin Woodman is Nick Chopper, commander of Ozma’s Army, who joins the rescue expedition, is transformed into an ornament and then a tin pig-shaped whistle, is restored by Billina, and returns to the Emerald City. In The Marvelous Land of Oz, the Tin-Woodman appears in the chapter list as the destination of a journey and as someone who plucks a rose.

Tin Woodman in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, 1900-04

Tin Woodman is a tin man and woodman made entirely of tin, with a jointed tin body, head, arms, and legs. He carries a sharp axe for chopping wood and an oil-can because rain or lack of oil can rust his joints. Found motionless beside a tree with rusted joints, he is oiled by Dorothy and the Scarecrow and joins them on the way to the Emerald City. His central wish is a heart from the Great Oz: after his former body was replaced with tin, he lost his heart and believes he cannot love the Munchkin girl he once loved and intends to marry. Though he says he lacks a heart, he is consistently careful not to harm living creatures, mourns an accidentally killed beetle, refuses to let the Lion kill a deer, saves the Queen of all the Field Mice by killing the Wildcat, and helps companions through forests, rivers, poppies, and dangers. His tin body protects him from the Lion's claws, poppy scent, and black bees, but he is battered by the Winged Monkeys and repaired by Winkie tinsmiths, who give him patches, a gold-handled axe, and a jeweled silver oil-can. He fights forty wolves, seeks Oz despite warnings that a heart may bring unhappiness, and ultimately receives a silk, sawdust-stuffed heart from Oz, installed through a square opening in his breast. He considers it lovely, kinder, and more tender than his former flesh heart. Afterward he weeps over Oz's departure, continues helping Dorothy with his axe and practical skills, and finally wants to return to the Winkies to rule them; Glinda promises he will be carried safely to the Country of the West.

Chapter V

The Tin Woodman is found standing motionless beside a partly chopped tree because his joints have rusted. After Dorothy and the Scarecrow oil him, he joins their party and uses his axe to clear a passage through thick trees. He wants the Great Oz to give him a heart so that he can return to the Munchkin girl and marry her.

Chapter VI

The Tin Woodman is unharmed when the Lion strikes him, though the Lion’s claws nearly become blunted against his tin body. He accidentally kills a beetle, cries with sorrow, and rusts his jaws until the Scarecrow oils them. He resolves to watch where he steps and avoid harming living creatures.

Chapter VII

The Tin Woodman chops wood for the campfire and refuses to let the Lion kill a deer because he would weep and his jaws would rust. He chops down the tree for the tree bridge, cuts it away during the Kalidah attack, and begins chopping small trees to build a raft.

Chapter VIII

The Tin Woodman helps finish and steer the raft, but he is separated from the Scarecrow when the current carries the raft downstream. He helps pull the raft to shore, searches for the Scarecrow, and later carries Dorothy with the Scarecrow through the poppies.

Chapter IX

The Tin Woodman kills the Wildcat with his axe to save the Queen of all the Field Mice. He builds a truck from tree limbs and logs, helps harness the mice, and assists in moving the Cowardly Lion out of the poppy bed.

Chapter X

The Tin Woodman continues with the group toward the Emerald City and says he wants the Great Oz to give him a heart. He receives a pair of locked green spectacles before entering the city.

Chapter XI

The Tin Woodman asks Oz for a heart so that he can love like other men. Oz promises him the biggest, kindest, and most loving heart in the Land of Oz after the Wicked Witch of the West is killed. He agrees to go with Dorothy and prepares by sharpening his axe and oiling his joints.

Chapter XII

The Tin Woodman kills all forty wolves with his sharp axe. He is unharmed when the black bees break their stings against his tin body, but the Winged Monkeys later drop him onto sharp rocks, leaving him battered and unable to move or groan.

Chapter XIII

The Tin Woodman is found battered and bent on a rocky plain, with his axe rusted and its handle broken. The Winkies carry him to the Yellow Castle, where tinsmiths repair him over three days and four nights. He receives a gold-handled axe and later leaves with Dorothy to seek his heart from Oz.

Chapter XIV

The Tin Woodman stays awake during the first night and grows impatient to reach Oz to receive his heart. He is initially frightened when the Winged Monkeys carry him, but soon rides cheerfully through the air and arrives safely at the Emerald City.

Chapter XV

The Tin Woodman demands the heart Oz promised him and angrily refuses to wait longer. He helps expose Oz by rushing at him with his axe, then learns that Oz believes he is fortunate not to have a heart. Oz nevertheless agrees to give him one.

Chapter XVI

The Tin Woodman visits Oz to receive his heart. Oz cuts a square hole in his breast, places a silk heart stuffed with sawdust inside, and solders the tin back together with a patch; the Tin Woodman is pleased and grateful.

Chapter XVIII

The Tin Woodman is grateful to Oz for giving him his heart and weeps over Oz's departure while Dorothy wipes away his tears so he will not rust. He agrees to travel with Dorothy to the Land of the South, believing his axe may be useful.

Chapter XIX

The Tin Woodman says that Oz gave him very good brains. When the trees attack the travelers, he chops through a branch, allowing the group to pass, and frees Toto from another branch.

Chapter XX

The Tin Woodman makes a ladder from wood so the travelers can climb over the first wall. He climbs over last and helps cross the Dainty China Country carefully. He says they must avoid hurting the china people because they may never recover from their injuries.

Chapter XXIII

The Tin Woodman wants to return to the Winkies and rule over them. Glinda promises that the Winged Monkeys will carry him safely to the Country of the West.

Tin Woodman in The Marvelous Land of Oz

The Marvelous Land of Oz, 1904-06

The Tin Woodman, also called Nick Chopper, is a tin man and woodman made of neatly soldered and riveted tin pieces in human form. A famous, successful hero, he is peaceful, skillful with an axe, fond of flowers, and values his heart above brains, education, or money. He says a good heart is the best part of a person; the heart he received from the Wizard of Oz is described as warm, admirable, and larger and warmer than most people's. After helping Dorothy destroy the Wicked Witch of the West, he is invited to rule the yellow Winkies' western country. As their Emperor, or Absolute Monarch, he is nickel-plated to cover scratches from adventures, bears an engraved star over the patch where his heart was placed, employs palace staff, and rules while remaining a loyal friend of Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Tip, and others. When the Scarecrow seeks his protection after losing the Emerald City, the Tin Woodman welcomes the party and joins its effort to recapture it. He provides practical care as well as courage: he tries not to harm sunflowers with girls' faces, repairs the Saw-Horse and Jack Pumpkinhead, calls on the Queen of the Field Mice, fights guards and Jackdaws with his axe, and helps companions escape imprisonment. As a mechanic, he builds the winged Thing from palm leaves and other materials, saves Jack during its flight, and helps protect the party after its crash. He travels on to Glinda's palace, supports the Scarecrow's appeal, and later unknowingly carries Mombi, transformed into a red rose, from the Emerald City in his buttonhole. He offers hospitality and free nickel-plating, then returns to the Kingdom of the Winkies, plans a new nickel coat, and makes the Scarecrow his Royal Treasurer. Throughout, his friendship, mechanical resourcefulness, courage, and insistence on the value of a good heart define him.

Tin Woodman in Ozma of Oz

Ozma of Oz, 1907

Tin Woodman, also called Nick Chopper, is an old friend of the author and readers, a companion and friend of Dorothy, a comrade of the Scarecrow, a faithful follower of Ozma, and a popular favorite among the people of Ev and the Land of Oz. A polished gentleman made of tin, with tin arms, an axe, and a continually beating heart, he considers himself superior to Tiktok in having a heart, while the Scarecrow has brains. He joins Ozma’s expedition as a soldier and commander of Ozma’s Army. Carrying his axe over his shoulder, he leads twenty-seven soldiers and commands twenty-six officers, describing an army with eight Generals, six Colonels, seven Majors, five Captains, and one private. He gently embraces Dorothy, thanks Tiktok for protecting her, urges the party onward, crosses beneath the giant’s raised mallet, and fits new ears to the Sawhorse. Though he fears the Nome King’s furnaces could melt his tin, he agrees to continue and says the Army of Oz will fight fiercely if needed. When Ozma is transformed in the Nome King’s palace, Tin Woodman claims the privilege, as commander, to follow and rescue her. After making a wrong guess, he is transformed into an ornament; the Nome King calls him the funniest ornament. The others cannot identify him among the palace ornaments, and he remains missing despite the Nome King’s knowledge of his form. He is eventually found to have become a small tin pig-shaped whistle in Evring’s pocket. Billina restores him by touching the whistle and saying “Ev.” He greets Dorothy and the Prince, remembers nothing of leaving the Nome King, returns to the Emerald City, is welcomed by Oz’s people, and receives a diamond-studded axe.
