Scarecrow in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, 1900-04

Scarecrow is a stuffed straw figure, made by a farmer two days before Dorothy finds him, placed on a tall stick in a cornfield, and abandoned there. His pole is stuck up his back; he has painted, unequal-sized blue eyes, a painted mouth, padded hands, and a head filled with straw. Because he is stuffed, he does not eat, sleep, tire, feel pain, or suffer from the poppy scent, and he can see in darkness. He fears fire and a lighted match because his straw could burn. Though he repeatedly believes he lacks brains and seeks them from the Great Oz, he often proves inventive: he helps free the Tin Woodman, devises ways across dangers, defeats forty crows, helps rescue the Cowardly Lion, and offers guidance to his companions. Dorothy removes him from his pole, and he travels with her toward the Emerald City to obtain brains. Along the way he is swept away on a pole, restored after the Winged Monkeys pull out his straw, and carried onward by the Winged Monkeys. After the Wicked Witch of the West is melted, he learns Oz is a humbug, but Oz fills his head with bran, pins, needles, and straw and tells him he must learn to use the brains himself. Scarecrow is pleased, considers himself wise, and says he has wonderful thoughts. Oz appoints him ruler of the Emerald City during Oz's absence, and the citizens call him the Wise Scarecrow. Grateful to Dorothy for his good luck, he leaves the city with her to seek Glinda's help, leading the group through further obstacles despite being thrown, flattened, and struck without serious harm. At the end, he plans to return to rule the Emerald City, with Glinda promising the Winged Monkeys will carry him over the Hammer-Heads' hill.

Chapter III

The Scarecrow is a stuffed figure placed in a cornfield to scare away crows. Dorothy lifts him from the pole stuck in his back, and he joins her journey to the Emerald City because he hopes Oz will give him brains.

Chapter IV

The Scarecrow frequently stumbles into holes in the uneven yellow bricks, though falling does not hurt him. He tells Dorothy how he was made two days earlier and placed in a cornfield, where crows revealed that he was stuffed with straw. He wants the Great Oz to give him brains.

Chapter V

The Scarecrow helps Dorothy free the Tin Woodman's rusted neck and arms. He continues toward the Emerald City to ask the Great Oz for brains, since his head is stuffed with straw. He argues that brains are preferable to a heart because a fool would not know what to do with a heart.

The Scarecrow is knocked spinning by the Lion but is not harmed because he is stuffed. He helps oil the Tin Woodman’s rusted jaws. He plans to ask Oz for brains because his head is stuffed with straw.

Chapter VII

The Scarecrow fills Dorothy's basket with nuts and covers her with dry leaves while she sleeps. He proposes using the Cowardly Lion to cross the first ditch, suggests cutting down a tree to bridge the second gulf, and devises the plan to chop away the tree bridge when the Kalidahs attack.

Chapter VIII

The Scarecrow is left clinging to his pole in the middle of the river when the current sweeps the raft away. A Stork carries him back to his friends, after which he happily joins the journey and helps carry Dorothy through the poppy field.

Chapter XI

The Scarecrow asks Oz to replace the straw in his head with brains. Oz promises him many good brains, making him the wisest man in the Land of Oz, if he helps kill the Wicked Witch of the West. The Scarecrow agrees to accompany Dorothy despite believing he will be of little help.

Chapter XII

The Scarecrow defeats the flock of forty crows by catching and twisting the necks of the birds. During the Winged Monkeys’ attack, the Monkeys pull all the straw from his clothes and head and throw his belongings into a tree.

Chapter XIV

The Scarecrow stays awake during the first night because he does not need sleep. He worries that the travelers have lost their way and that he will never reach Oz to get his brains, but the Winged Monkeys later carry him to the Emerald City.

Chapter XV

The Scarecrow tells the Guardian of the Gates that the Wicked Witch of the West has melted and presses Oz to honor his promise. He discovers that Oz is a humbug and is promised that his head will be stuffed with brains, though he must learn how to use them himself.

The Scarecrow visits Oz to receive his brains. Oz fills his head with bran mixed with pins and needles, along with straw, and the Scarecrow returns pleased and believing he will know everything.

Scarecrow in The Marvelous Land of Oz

The Marvelous Land of Oz, 1904-06

Scarecrow is a non-human, straw-made scarecrow and monarch: the King and rightful ruler of the Emerald City, also described as a ruler of Oz. He sits on a glittering throne in the royal palace, wears faded blue clothes and a heavy jeweled golden crown, and has a painted sack head and straw-filled body. Though carelessly stuffed, he is renowned as the wisest personage in the Land of Oz because of brains originally given by the Wizard of Oz. He values those brains, uses them to solve problems, and is a famous, successful hero. His closest recurring companions include Dorothy, Tip, and especially the Tin Woodman. After becoming a popular king following the Wizard’s disappearance, he rules the Emerald City until General Jinjur’s girls conquer it. Threatened with being turned into a rag carpet and cushions, he calmly escapes with Tip and Jack Pumpkinhead on the Saw-Horse to seek the Tin Woodman. Water soaks and shifts his straw, but Tip dries and restores him; later the Tin Woodman helps clean and repair him. Returning toward the city, Scarecrow guides his party through dangers, carries mice in his straw, and uses them to frighten the Army of Revolt and free the captured travelers. Recognizing that this does not truly retake the city, he plans an aerial escape in the Gump and seeks Glinda the Good’s help. During the journey, Jackdaws pull out all his straw, so his friends fill him with money, yet he continues to rely on the same brains. Before Glinda, he declares that Ozma is the rightful heir and that he would surrender the throne to her. He joins Glinda’s forces in entering the Emerald City and searching for Mombi. By the end, he becomes the Tin Woodman’s Royal Treasurer, maintains that brains are worth more than money, and remains closely joined to the Tin Woodman.

Scarecrow in Ozma of Oz

Ozma of Oz, 1907

Scarecrow is a straw-stuffed person from Oz: a painted, straw-padded figure whose soft body can cushion a fall, but whose straw makes fire a grave danger. Though once a ruler of the Land of Oz and later deposed, he is a popular favorite, Dorothy's old friend and companion, and an old comrade of the Tin Woodman. He traveled with Dorothy to the Wizard of Oz to obtain brains; he now has excellent brains, thinks despite his straw-filled head, and says they work splendidly. His very active conscience, loyalty, and concern for his companions matter as much as his intelligence. He arrives with Ozma on the wooden Saw-Horse, recognizes and warmly embraces Dorothy, and joins the expedition as Ozma's adviser. He fears the Nome King's furnaces but continues onward. He keeps Billina's egg, devises a way beneath the Giant's raised hammer, crosses on the Sawhorse, and is struck on the left foot while saving the private. In the Nome King's palace he worries about being turned into bric-a-brac, grieves when the Tin Woodman becomes an ornament, seeks to protect Dorothy, and retrieves Billina's egg from beneath the throne. After failing to identify the enchantments, he becomes a solid-gold card-receiver, then is restored by Billina. Still fearing fire, Scarecrow follows Billina's plan and throws two eggs into the Nome King's eyes, driving off the Nome warriors and blinding the King. He presses for the Tin Woodman's recovery, commands the army during the journey, and is heartbroken when his comrade seems left behind. When the Tin Woodman is restored, he embraces him. Scarecrow returns with Ozma's party to the Emerald City, where the people greet him joyfully; he receives complexion powder and sits at the feast without needing food.
