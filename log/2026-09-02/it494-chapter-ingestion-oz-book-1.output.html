IT494: a document, unit by unit¶

# Each unit of a document (here a chapter of a public-domain novel; a chat session works the same) becomes entities, quote-backed facts, a summary, and per-entity cells, in its own workspace with no identity resolved. Every fact carries a verbatim quote; a quote that is not in the text is dropped and counted. Then, with the whole document in evidence, the major entities are reconciled bottom up, a store is built over the major entities alone, the predicate vocabulary is consolidated, and the graph is drawn. Nothing in the prompts knows what a novel is; the only corpus-specific cell is the loader. # To run: attach the units dataset, add an OPENAI_API_KEY secret, turn Internet on, Run All. #

2 · The model¶

One function makes every call and logs its cost. One gate checks that a string the model claims to have copied is really in the unit.

import requests
from kaggle_secrets import UserSecretsClient

MODEL = "gpt-5.6-luna"                # extraction
JUDGE = "gpt-5.6-terra"               # reconciliation and vocabulary verdicts
PRICE = {"gpt-5.6-luna": (0.20, 1.20), "gpt-5.6-terra": (2.00, 12.00)}   # $ per M tokens in, out
SPEND_STOP = 8.00                     # dollars; the run halts past this
KEY = UserSecretsClient().get_secret("OPENAI_API_KEY")
calls = []

def spend():
    return sum(c["cost"] for c in calls)

def generate(prompt, stage, model=MODEL, effort="low"):
    """One JSON-mode call; the reply parsed, the cost logged."""
    if spend() >= SPEND_STOP:
        raise RuntimeError(f"spending stop: ${spend():.2f}")
    t0 = time.time()
    r = requests.post("https://api.openai.com/v1/chat/completions",
                      headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {KEY}"}, timeout=180,
                      json={"model": model, "reasoning_effort": effort,
                            "response_format": {"type": "json_object"},
                            "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": prompt}]})
    if r.status_code != 200:
        raise RuntimeError(f"OpenAI {r.status_code}: {r.text[:300]}")
    body = r.json()
    u, (p_in, p_out) = body["usage"], PRICE[model]
    calls.append({"stage": stage, "model": body["model"], "in": u["prompt_tokens"],
                  "out": u["completion_tokens"], "seconds": round(time.time() - t0, 1),
                  "cost": (u["prompt_tokens"] * p_in + u["completion_tokens"] * p_out) / 1e6})
    return json.loads(body["choices"][0]["message"]["content"])

def in_text(text, s):
    """The gate: the string occurs in the unit, line wraps forgiven."""
    return bool(s.split()) and re.search(r"\s+".join(re.escape(w) for w in s.split()), text) is not None

Entities, then facts about them, then the summary and cells. Each prompt starts with the unit text so the three calls share a cached prefix.

def entity_prompt(unit):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document. Identify the entities it involves: each person, group, place, thing, event, or topic that acts, is acted upon, or is discussed in its own right, plus any named person, place, group, or thing, however briefly mentioned. Parts, components, and possessions of a listed entity are not entities; they belong inside that entity's facts.

Return JSON {{"entities": [{{"name", "named", "kind", "salience", "surface_forms"}}]}} where:
- name: for a named entity, the fullest name the text uses; for an unnamed one, a head word plus a parenthetical anchoring it to a named entity, like "car (Sam's car)"
- named: true if the text gives it a proper name
- kind: one lowercase word: person, group, place, object, event, topic, or another if none fits
- salience: "major" only if it would appear in a two-sentence summary of this text, else "minor"
- surface_forms: every distinct verbatim string the text uses to refer to it, bare pronouns excluded"""

def fact_prompt(unit, names, majors):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document; below, the entities identified in it. For each entity, distill every durable fact the text states about it: what it is, its attributes, its states, its situation, its parts and possessions, its relationships to the other listed entities. Not moment-to-moment actions or passing remarks; a lasting disposition, habit, or position counts.

Return JSON {{"facts": [{{"subject", "predicate", "object", "qualifiers", "quote"}}]}} where:
- subject: a name from ENTITIES, exactly as written
- predicate: a lowercase_snake_case relation in the present tense, named the way the text gives it
- object: another entity's name exactly as written when the fact relates two entities, else a short literal value; never a bare true or false
- qualifiers: a short phrase for role, manner, or condition, else null
- quote: one verbatim substring of the text that supports the fact, copied exactly
- each fact is atomic: one attribute or one relationship, with a bare value rather than a phrase
- a relationship between a major entity and a minor one is stated once, under the major entity; only between two major entities may it be stated from both sides

ENTITIES: {", ".join(names)}
MAJOR: {", ".join(majors)}"""

def cells_prompt(unit, named):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document. Write:
1. "summary": the unit in 3-5 concrete sentences.
2. "cells": for each entity below that appears, 1-3 sentences in third person on what it does, what happens to it, or what is learned about it in this unit, written so it can be appended to that entity's running record.
Use only what the text says.

Return JSON {{"summary": "...", "cells": [{{"entity", "text"}}]}}

ENTITIES: {", ".join(named)}"""

def derive(unit):
    text = unit["text"]
    entities, dropped = [], []
    for e in generate(entity_prompt(unit), "entities")["entities"]:
        e["surface_forms"] = [s for s in e["surface_forms"] if in_text(text, s)]
        (entities if e["surface_forms"] else dropped).append(e)
    names = [e["name"] for e in entities]
    majors = [e["name"] for e in entities if e["salience"] == "major"]

    facts, lost = [], []
    for f in generate(fact_prompt(unit, names, majors), "facts")["facts"]:
        if f["subject"] in names and in_text(text, f["quote"]):
            f["object_is_entity"] = f["object"] in names
            facts.append(f)
        else:
            lost.append(f)

    out = generate(cells_prompt(unit, [e["name"] for e in entities if e["named"]]), "cells")
    cells = [c for c in out["cells"] if c["entity"] in names]
    return {"unit": unit["label"], "position": unit["position"], "entities": entities,
            "dropped": dropped, "facts": facts, "lost": lost, "summary": out["summary"], "cells": cells}

def show(rec, full=False):
    print("=" * 88)
    print(f"{rec['unit']}: {len(rec['entities'])} entities, {len(rec['facts'])} facts, "
          f"{len(rec['dropped'])} entities and {len(rec['lost'])} facts dropped; run so far ${spend():.3f}")
    for e in rec["entities"]:
        print(f"  {e['name']:<36} {e['kind']:<9} {'named' if e['named'] else 'unnamed':<8} {e['salience']}")
    if not full:
        return
    print(f"\nFACTS ({len(rec['facts'])})")
    for f in rec["facts"]:
        q = f"  [{f['qualifiers']}]" if f["qualifiers"] else ""
        print(f"  {f['subject']} -{f['predicate']}-> {f['object']}{q}")
        print(f"      \"{' '.join(f['quote'].split())[:100]}\"")
    print("\nSUMMARY\n" + rec["summary"])
    for c in rec["cells"]:
        print(f"\n[{c['entity']}]\n{c['text']}")

8 · The store¶

Nodes are the major entities. Every fact whose subject resolved to a major entity is kept under that node: an object that is another major entity is an edge; any other object, a minor entity or a literal, is a property. Minor entities are not nodes and have no id, and a fact between two minors is dropped here; both stay in the unit records as context.

9 · Consolidate the predicates, flag the forks¶

The model named relations as each unit put them, so the vocabulary has diverged. Over the store's facts: every surface predicate is tallied with its sample objects and qualifiers, and one judge call groups the names that mean the same relation under a canonical name. A single name used for two clearly different relations is a fork candidate: flagged with its two senses, never split here. Facts keep the predicate the model wrote and gain the canonical one.

Dorothy  (24 units)
Dorothy is an orphaned, innocent, well-grown child who lives with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry on the Kansas prairies. She dearly loves and regularly plays with her little dog Toto, and her laughter brightens their household. A cyclone carries her house, with Toto inside, to a beautiful country, where its landing kills the Wicked Witch of the East. Dorothy receives the Witch's Silver Shoes and the Good Witch of the North's protective kiss, then follows the yellow brick road toward the Emerald City to ask Oz to send her home.

On the journey, Dorothy is practical, kind, and protective: she rescues the Scarecrow, frees and oils the Tin Woodman, defends Toto from the Cowardly Lion, and welcomes all three as companions. She endures hunger, rivers, poppies, and dangerous travel, reaching Oz only to be required to kill the Wicked Witch of the West. Protected by the kiss, she is enslaved in the Witch's kitchen, but melts the Witch with water, frees the Lion, and helps rescue her friends.

Oz cannot return her; a balloon leaves without her. Dorothy travels south to Glinda, gives her the Golden Cap, learns the Silver Shoes' power, bids farewell to her companions, and returns to Kansas with Toto, joyfully rejoining Aunt Em.

Toto  (22 units)
Toto is Dorothy’s little black dog and companion, with long silky hair, lively small black eyes, and a wee nose. He belongs to Dorothy, travels with her throughout her journey and homecoming, plays and chases animals, eats a little of everything at supper, and usually sleeps beside her. Though unable to speak, he barks, whimpers, howls, and sometimes bites or snaps: he fears the Witch of the North, initially distrusts the Scarecrow and Lion, attacks the Tin Woodman’s legs and the Witch after she strikes him, and later becomes the Lion’s good friend. During the cyclone he hides under a bed, nearly falls through the trap door, and is pulled back by Dorothy. He can see in darkness, is carried across obstacles, on the raft, through the poppy field, and by Winged Monkeys; the poppies put him to sleep. In the Emerald City he wears locked green spectacles, later a ribbon and bell, and receives a golden collar from the Winkies. He faces bees, captivity, fatigue, a branch that traps him, and separation from Dorothy when the balloon rises. Finally, he travels in the Silver Shoes to Kansas, runs toward the barn barking, and returns home with Dorothy.

Scarecrow  (21 units)
The Scarecrow is a stuffed man made by a farmer only two days before Dorothy meets him, originally placed on a pole in a cornfield to frighten crows. His faded blue suit and pointed blue hat cover a body and head filled with straw; he has a painted mouth, is neither hungry nor tired, can see in darkness, and is unharmed by falls, though he fears fire. Believing straw leaves him unable to think, he values brains above a heart and joins Dorothy’s journey to the Emerald City to ask Oz for them. Despite calling himself brainless, he repeatedly devises plans: crossing gulfs, defeating forty crows, rescuing the Lion with the Field Mice, and guiding the travelers. He is swept into a river, restored after the Winged Monkeys remove his straw and clothes, and later receives Oz’s promised brains, containing bran, pins, and needles. Oz appoints him ruler of the Emerald City, where people consider him wise and like him. Loyal to Dorothy, he leaves his rule to help her reach the Land of the South, leads the group, survives attacks by fighting trees and a Hammer-Head, and is carried over the hill by Winged Monkeys.

Kansas  (19 units)
Kansas is Dorothy’s home country and the home of Aunt Em and Uncle Henry, containing broad prairies and gray, dreary homes. Dorothy describes it as civilized but dry and gray; despite its dullness, she believes people prefer it to any other country because there is no place like home. Throughout the journey, Kansas is Dorothy and Toto’s desired destination: Dorothy repeatedly asks or plans to ask the Great Oz to send them back, worries obstacles such as the river will prevent her return, and becomes sad when returning seems increasingly difficult. Aunt Em and Uncle Henry remain there, and Dorothy dreams of Aunt Em welcoming her home. Oz wants to return with Dorothy but does not know its direction, and the Winged Monkeys cannot take her there. Dorothy continues seeking another route, even considering bringing the china Princess to Aunt Em’s mantel. Ultimately, the Silver Shoes carry Dorothy back to Kansas in three steps, depositing her on the broad prairie near Uncle Henry’s new farmhouse.

Tin Woodman  (19 units)
The Tin Woodman is entirely tin, not flesh, with a hinged head and an axe he uses for chopping wood, trees, enemies, and obstacles. Formerly flesh, he loved a Munchkin girl and planned to marry her, but after becoming tin he lacked a heart and believed this prevented love; he seeks a heart from Oz, valuing it above brains. Careful not to harm living creatures, he mourns even a beetle and rusts when crying, requiring oil. He nevertheless kills a Wildcat to save the Field Mice and forty wolves defending his companions, protects the mice from Toto, and is immune to black-bee stings. He helps the travelers build bridges, rafts, and a mouse-drawn truck, crosses hazards, rescues companions, and carries Dorothy while unaffected by poppies. Dropped by Winged Monkeys, he is dented and immobilized, then repaired by Winkie tinsmiths; his axe is improved with a gold handle. After demanding Oz's promised heart, he receives one, finds it kinder than his former fleshly heart, and avoids rusting as he mourns Oz. He joins Dorothy's southward journey, guards the group, overcomes fighting trees, and proposes a ladder for the china wall. The Winkies intend him to rule them.

Cowardly Lion  (18 units)
The Cowardly Lion is a large, heavy wild beast, regarded by other animals as King of Beasts but convinced by his own nature that he is a coward who lacks courage and fears danger, people, falling, and especially Kalidahs. Dorothy stops his initial attack on her companions, after which he becomes her friend and traveling companion, seeking courage from Oz while protecting her and driving off other wild beasts. Though afraid, he repeatedly acts bravely: he carries the party over a ditch, confronts Kalidahs with his roar, pulls their raft through a river, and frightens Winkie attackers. Poppy scent puts him to sleep, but field mice save him. Captured by Winged Monkeys, he is imprisoned by the Wicked Witch until Dorothy frees him; he refuses to be harnessed. Oz explains that facing danger while afraid is true courage and gives him confidence. He thereafter fears nothing and considers himself exceptionally brave. He accompanies Dorothy south, dislikes city life, kills the great spider, and is made King of the Forest, promising to rule after Dorothy departs for Kansas. He is also carried by Winged Monkeys when needed, including over the Hammer-Heads’ hill.

Oz  (17 units)
Oz, addressed as the Great and Terrible Wizard, rules Emerald City wisely and is believed more powerful than witches and wizards. DOCUMENT
The document follows Dorothy, a Kansas girl carried away with her dog Toto when a cyclone lifts their farmhouse and deposits it in the Land of Oz. There, her house kills the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing the Munchkins, and the Witch of the North gives Dorothy the dead Witch’s Silver Shoes and directs her to seek the Wizard of Oz in the Emerald City. Traveling on the yellow brick road, Dorothy is joined by the Scarecrow, who wants brains; the Tin Woodman, who wants a heart; and the Cowardly Lion, who wants courage. The companions face forests, ditches, dangerous creatures, a river, deadly poppies, and other obstacles, repeatedly helping one another reach the Emerald City. Oz promises to grant their wishes only if they destroy the Wicked Witch of the West, so they journey into the Winkies’ country. After the Witch’s forces capture or damage her friends, Dorothy melts the Witch with water, frees the Lion and the Winkies, and helps restore the Scarecrow and Tin Woodman. Back in the Emerald City, the travelers learn that Oz is an ordinary, frightened old man who relies on tricks, though he gives the three companions symbolic gifts that satisfy them. Oz’s attempt to take Dorothy home by balloon fails when Toto delays her, leaving Dorothy in Oz while the Scarecrow becomes ruler of the Emerald City. Dorothy and her friends then travel south to seek Glinda, overcoming attacking trees, the China Country, a forest monster, and the Hammer-Heads. Glinda reveals that Dorothy’s Silver Shoes have always had the power to take her home, uses the Golden Cap to send Dorothy’s friends to their respective kingdoms, and Dorothy returns to Kansas with Toto, where Aunt Em welcomes her home.
