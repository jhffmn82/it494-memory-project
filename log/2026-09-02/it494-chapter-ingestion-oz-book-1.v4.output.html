IT494: a document, unit by unit¶

# Each unit of a document (here a chapter of a public-domain novel; a chat session works the same) becomes entities, quote-backed facts, a summary, and per-entity cells, in its own workspace with no identity resolved. Every fact carries a verbatim quote; a quote that is not in the text is dropped and counted. Then, with the whole document in evidence, the major entities are reconciled bottom up, a store is built over the major entities alone, the predicate vocabulary is consolidated, and the graph is drawn. Nothing in the prompts knows what a novel is; the only corpus-specific cell is the loader. # To run: attach the units dataset, add an OPENAI_API_KEY secret, turn Internet on, Run All. #

2 · The model¶

One function makes every call and logs its cost. One gate checks that a string the model claims to have copied is really in the unit.

import requests
from kaggle_secrets import UserSecretsClient

MODEL = "gpt-5.6-luna"                # extraction
JUDGE = "gpt-5.6-terra"               # reconciliation and vocabulary verdicts
PRICE = {"gpt-5.6-luna": (0.20, 1.20), "gpt-5.6-terra": (2.00, 12.00)}   # $ per M tokens in, out
SPEND_STOP = 8.00                     # dollars; the run halts past this
KEY = UserSecretsClient().get_secret("OPENAI_API_KEY")
calls = []

def spend():
    return sum(c["cost"] for c in calls)

def generate(prompt, stage, model=MODEL, effort="low"):
    """One JSON-mode call; the reply parsed, the cost logged."""
    if spend() >= SPEND_STOP:
        raise RuntimeError(f"spending stop: ${spend():.2f}")
    t0 = time.time()
    r = requests.post("https://api.openai.com/v1/chat/completions",
                      headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {KEY}"}, timeout=180,
                      json={"model": model, "reasoning_effort": effort,
                            "response_format": {"type": "json_object"},
                            "messages": [{"role": "user", "content": prompt}]})
    if r.status_code != 200:
        raise RuntimeError(f"OpenAI {r.status_code}: {r.text[:300]}")
    body = r.json()
    u, (p_in, p_out) = body["usage"], PRICE[model]
    calls.append({"stage": stage, "model": body["model"], "in": u["prompt_tokens"],
                  "out": u["completion_tokens"], "seconds": round(time.time() - t0, 1),
                  "cost": (u["prompt_tokens"] * p_in + u["completion_tokens"] * p_out) / 1e6})
    return json.loads(body["choices"][0]["message"]["content"])

def in_text(text, s):
    """The gate: the string occurs in the unit, line wraps forgiven."""
    return bool(s.split()) and re.search(r"\s+".join(re.escape(w) for w in s.split()), text) is not None

3 · The prompts¶

Entities, then facts about them, then the summary and cells. Each prompt starts with the unit text so the three calls share a cached prefix.

def entity_prompt(unit):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document. Identify the entities it involves: each person, group, place, thing, event, or topic that acts, is acted upon, or is discussed in its own right, plus any named person, place, group, or thing, however briefly mentioned. Parts, components, and possessions of a listed entity are not entities; they belong inside that entity's facts.

Return JSON {{"entities": [{{"name", "named", "kind", "salience", "surface_forms"}}]}} where:
- name: for a named entity, the fullest name the text uses; for an unnamed one, a head word plus a parenthetical anchoring it to a named entity, like "car (Sam's car)"
- named: true if the text gives it a proper name
- kind: one lowercase word: person, group, place, object, event, topic, or another if none fits
- salience: "major" only if it would appear in a two-sentence summary of this text, else "minor"; an event or topic is major only if the text returns to it or it changes what follows
- surface_forms: every distinct verbatim string the text uses to refer to it, bare pronouns excluded"""

def fact_prompt(unit, names, majors):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document; below, the entities identified in it. For each entity, distill every durable fact the text states about it: what it is, its attributes, its states, its situation, its parts and possessions, its relationships to the other listed entities. Not moment-to-moment actions or passing remarks; a lasting disposition, habit, or position counts.

Return JSON {{"facts": [{{"subject", "predicate", "object", "qualifiers", "quote"}}]}} where:
- subject: a name from ENTITIES, exactly as written
- predicate: a lowercase_snake_case relation in the present tense, named the way the text gives it
- object: another entity's name exactly as written when the fact relates two entities, else a short literal value; never a bare true or false. The object carries the value and never repeats the predicate
- qualifiers: a short phrase for role, manner, or condition, else null
- quote: one verbatim substring of the text that supports the fact, copied exactly
- each fact is atomic: one attribute or one relationship, with a bare value rather than a phrase
- a relationship between a major entity and a minor one is stated once, under the major entity; only between two major entities may it be stated from both sides

ENTITIES: {", ".join(names)}
MAJOR: {", ".join(majors)}"""

def cells_prompt(unit, named):
    return f"""TEXT ({unit['label']}):
{unit['text']}

Above is one unit of a longer document. Write:
1. "summary": the unit in 3-5 concrete sentences.
2. "cells": for each entity below that appears, 1-3 sentences in third person on what it does, what happens to it, or what is learned about it in this unit, written so it can be appended to that entity's running record.
Use only what the text says.

Return JSON {{"summary": "...", "cells": [{{"entity", "text"}}]}}

ENTITIES: {", ".join(named)}"""

4 · Derive one unit¶

Three calls, two gates, one record per unit. Nothing is shared between units.

def derive(unit):
    text = unit["text"]
    entities, dropped = [], []
    for e in generate(entity_prompt(unit), "entities")["entities"]:
        e["surface_forms"] = [s for s in e["surface_forms"] if in_text(text, s)]
        (entities if e["surface_forms"] else dropped).append(e)
    names = [e["name"] for e in entities]
    majors = [e["name"] for e in entities if e["salience"] == "major"]

    facts, lost = [], []
    for f in generate(fact_prompt(unit, names, majors), "facts")["facts"]:
        if f["subject"] in names and in_text(text, f["quote"]):
            f["object_is_entity"] = f["object"] in names
            facts.append(f)
        else:
            lost.append(f)

    out = generate(cells_prompt(unit, [e["name"] for e in entities if e["named"]]), "cells")
    cells = [c for c in out["cells"] if c["entity"] in names]
    return {"unit": unit["label"], "position": unit["position"], "entities": entities,
            "dropped": dropped, "facts": facts, "lost": lost, "summary": out["summary"], "cells": cells}

def show(rec, full=False):
    print("=" * 88)
    print(f"{rec['unit']}: {len(rec['entities'])} entities, {len(rec['facts'])} facts, "
          f"{len(rec['dropped'])} entities and {len(rec['lost'])} facts dropped; run so far ${spend():.3f}")
    for e in rec["entities"]:
        print(f"  {e['name']:<36} {e['kind']:<9} {'named' if e['named'] else 'unnamed':<8} {e['salience']}")
    if not full:
        return
    print(f"\nFACTS ({len(rec['facts'])})")
    for f in rec["facts"]:
        q = f"  [{f['qualifiers']}]" if f["qualifiers"] else ""
        print(f"  {f['subject']} -{f['predicate']}-> {f['object']}{q}")
        print(f"      \"{' '.join(f['quote'].split())[:100]}\"")
    print("\nSUMMARY\n" + rec["summary"])
    for c in rec["cells"]:
        print(f"\n[{c['entity']}]\n{c['text']}")

    """One call over up to ten pairs of cluster roots; returns verdicts in batch order."""
    prompt = JUDGE_PROMPT + "\n\n".join(f"PAIR {k}\nA:\n{dossier(ra)}\n\nB:\n{dossier(rb)}"
                                        for k, (ra, rb) in enumerate(batch, 1))
    out = generate(prompt, "judge", model=JUDGE, effort="medium")
    verdicts = {}
    for v in out["verdicts"]:
        try:
            verdicts[int(v["pair"]) - 1] = (v["verdict"], v["reason"])
        except (KeyError, TypeError, ValueError):
            pass
    return verdicts

8 · The store¶

The document and each of its units are nodes; so is every major entity. A major entity appears_in the document and in every unit it was found in, and carries its cells in reading order: the column of the cell matrix, whose rows are the unit summaries on the document node. Every text fact whose subject resolved to a major entity is kept under that node: an object that is another major entity is an edge; any other object, a minor entity or a literal, is a property. Minor entities are not nodes and have no id, and a fact between two minors is dropped here; both stay in the unit records as context.

9 · Consolidate the predicates, flag the forks¶

The model named relations as each unit put them, so the vocabulary has diverged. Over the store's facts: every surface predicate is tallied with its sample objects and qualifiers, and one judge call groups the names that mean the same relation under a canonical name. A single name used for two clearly different relations is a fork candidate: flagged with its two senses, never split here. Facts keep the predicate the model wrote and gain the canonical one.

Dorothy  (24 units)
Dorothy is an orphaned little girl from Kansas who lives with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry and dearly loves Toto, her dog and constant companion. Innocent and harmless, she is carried with Toto inside her house by a cyclone to the Land of Oz, where the house unintentionally kills the Wicked Witch of the East. The Witch of the North calls her a sorceress, gives her the dead Witch’s Silver Shoes, and protects her with a kiss on her forehead. Dorothy’s central aim is to return with Toto to Kansas, Aunt Em, and Uncle Henry.

Following the yellow brick road toward the Emerald City, she gathers provisions, frees the Scarecrow, oils and recruits the Tin Woodman, defends Toto from and befriends the Cowardly Lion, and travels with all three companions. She endures hunger, ditches, rivers, poppies that put her to sleep, and other dangers, while receiving help from the Stork, field mice, and Emerald City residents. Oz agrees to help her only if she kills the Wicked Witch of the West. In the West, Dorothy is forced to work but secretly aids the imprisoned Lion; she melts the Witch with water, frees the Lion and Winkies, rescues her other friends, and acquires the Golden Cap.

Back in Emerald City, she exposes Oz as “The Great and Terrible Humbug,” but his balloon attempt fails when she misses its launch while seeking Toto. She then journeys south with her companions to Glinda, using the Golden Cap and Winged Monkeys when necessary, showing care for injured companions and refusing to take the china Princess from her home. At Glinda’s Castle, Dorothy gives Glinda the Cap, says farewell to her friends, and uses the Silver Shoes to return to Kansas with Toto, joyfully rejoining Aunt Em.

Toto  (21 units)
Toto is Dorothy’s little black dog, a real meat dog rather than tin or stuffed, with long silky hair, small black eyes, and a funny wee nose. He belongs with Dorothy, lives in Kansas before their journey, plays all day, makes her laugh, and cannot speak. During the cyclone he hides under a bed, falls through an open trap door, and Dorothy pulls him back. In the new land he follows Dorothy, whines on waking, barks after the Witch of the North vanishes, and accompanies her throughout her travels. Munchkins treat him kindly, though he initially distrusts and growls at the Scarecrow. He barks at birds, chases birds, squirrels, field mice, moths, butterflies, and a kitten, jumps broken yellow-brick gaps, sees well in darkness, and sleeps beside Dorothy. He snaps at the Tin Woodman’s legs and hurts his teeth, confronts the Lion until Dorothy protects him, and later becomes the Cowardly Lion’s friend. He is carried across obstacles and by Winged Monkeys, falls asleep from the poppy scent, is rescued with Dorothy, and is restrained from frightening the field-mouse Queen. In Emerald City he receives locked green spectacles; later Dorothy adorns him with a ribbon and bell, and the Winkies give him a golden collar. He stays near Dorothy through her forced work, unhappy when she cries, grows too tired to chase a butterfly, is freed from a branch by the Tin Woodman, and is quieted in the china country. During the balloon launch, chasing a kitten causes him and Dorothy to miss the balloon. He finally returns with Dorothy to Kansas, runs toward the barn barking, and is home again.

Scarecrow  (21 units)
The Scarecrow is a straw-stuffed figure made by a Munchkin farmer and mounted on a stake in a cornfield to keep birds from corn. He wears a worn blue suit, pointed blue hat, and old boots with blue tops; his eyes, ears, and mouth are painted. Because his head and body contain straw, he believes he lacks brains, cannot think, and feels foolish, so he seeks brains from the Great Oz in Emerald City. He neither hungers nor eats, does not tire walking, can see in darkness, but fears fire and lighted matches because they could burn him.

He joins Dorothy and later the Tin Woodman on the road to Emerald City. Though he often falls into holes and needs help up, he is unharmed and cheerful. He helps free the Tin Woodman, gathers nuts, protects Dorothy while she sleeps, and develops clever plans for crossing ditches and defeating the Kalidahs. He organizes the mice’s rescue of the Lion from the poppy bed and speaks for the party at Emerald City’s gate.

The Witch’s crows are defeated when he twists their necks, but Winged Monkeys pull out his straw, toss his clothes into a tree, and leave him dismantled. Dorothy has him restuffed with clean straw. After Dorothy melts the Witch and Oz is exposed as a humbug, Oz fulfills his promise by supplying him with brains, after which he claims to have wonderful thoughts.

Oz appoints him ruler of Emerald City, where he is liked by the people, but he remains grateful and loyal to Dorothy and first helps her journey home. He leads the travelers through further dangers: fighting trees toss him without hurting him; he cushions their descent from a wall, is flattened and restuffed; he keeps night watch; and a Hammer-Head knocks him down a hill. Finally, intending to return to rule Emerald City, he is promised transport by Winged Monkeys over the Hammer-Heads’ hill.

Kansas  (20 units)
Kansas is Dorothy and Toto’s civilized homeland and the place Dorothy consistently regards as home. It is a broad, flat prairie, great, dry, and gray, with land, grass, and Dorothy’s house made dull by sun, wind, and rain; at first, no trees or other houses are visible around the home. Though Dorothy calls it dreary, she says people of flesh and blood prefer it to any other country, however beautiful. The Witch of the North has never heard of Kansas and says civilized countries are believed to lack witches, wizards, sorceresses, and magicians. Dorothy contrasts its ordinary scarecrows, which never wink, with those she encounters elsewhere. Kansas is associated with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry, and Toto’s former mouse-chasing life there. Throughout the journey, Dorothy repeatedly seeks Oz’s help to return to Kansas, worrying as obstacles, separation from her route, uncertainty about its location and direction, Oz’s inability to send her, and the uncrossable desert delay her. She dreams of reunion with Aunt Em, considers bringing the china Princess there, and is supported by the Lion’s intention to see her safely on her way. Finally, the Silver Shoes carry Dorothy home in three steps, depositing her on the broad Kansas prairie near a new farmhouse, where Aunt Em awaits her.

Tin Woodman  (19 units)
The Tin Woodman is a wholly tin, jointed traveler with an axe and oil-can, vulnerable to rust when wet or crying and convinced that lacking a heart prevents happiness. Once the son of a woodman and caregiver to his mother until her death, he had intended to marry a Munchkin girl; the Wicked Witch of the East caused his body to be replaced by tin, leaving him without his former heart. Rusted beside a tree for over a year, he is freed when Dorothy and the Scarecrow oil him, becomes Dorothy’s friend, and joins the journey to the Emerald City to seek a heart from Oz. Though he believes himself heartless, he is careful not to harm living creatures and repeatedly aids companions: clearing roads, making bridges, defeating Kalidahs, saving the Queen of the Field Mice, assisting the Lion, and guarding the group. He kills forty wolves and withstands bees, but Winged Monkeys batter and bend him on rocks.
